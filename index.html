Key Experience

Mapping the Evolving Landscape of EV Battery Chemistries and Supply Chain Vulnerabilities

Led a pioneering analysis of EV battery chemistries across the US, Europe, and China (2022–2024), examining how adoption of NMC, NCA, and LFP cathodes influences EV performance, manufacturing trends, and global supply chain dependencies. Revealed region-specific vulnerabilities—such as the US’s heavy reliance on imported high-nickel batteries and China’s dominance in LFP-driven, cost-efficient production. 

 Global Urban Travel Shift and Electrification Model 

Worked GUTSEM to project the future of light-duty vehicle (LDV) fleets under various scenarios—ranging from business-as-usual to high public transport adoption and EV uptake. The model evaluates fuel economy trends, infrastructure costs, and vehicle stock dynamics across ICEs, EVs, and autonomous vehicles.  Our analysis helps estimate future vehicle demand, energy use, emissions, and battery needs—guiding both public and private sector decisions in the transition to sustainable urban mobility.

Economics of PEM and Alkaline Electrolyzers : A compartive study for Hydrogen Production in Germany and India with solar integration

Conducted a comparative economic analysis of Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) and Alkaline electrolyzers for hydrogen production with solar integration in Germany and India. The project forecasted significant reductions in the levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) by 2050, with projected costs of $0.52/kg for PEM and $0.68/kg for alkaline electrolyzers.

Techno-Economic Assessment of Hydrogen via Natural Gas (SMR)

Conducted a techno-economic analysis of hydrogen production using Steam Methane Reforming (SMR) in an India-specific context. The study estimated hydrogen production costs at $2.05/kg without carbon sequestration and $3.33/kg with sequestration. Utilized sensitivity analysis and Monte Carlo simulations to identify key cost drivers, highlighting feedstock consumption and natural gas pricing as primary influences on production economics.

Nuclear based Hydrogen Production: Economic Insights

Leveraging IAEA’s Hydrogen Economic Evaluation Program (HEEP), we examined nuclear-powered hydrogen production by combining Pressurized Water Reactors (PWR) with conventional electrolysis. Calculating Levelized cost of Hydrogen, our results suggest economic feasibility in specific regions. South Korea leads at $2.74/kg, while US and India lag at $4.12/kg and $3.31/kg. Variations in overnight capital costs among nations influence the feasibility.
